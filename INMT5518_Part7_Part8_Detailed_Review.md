# SECTION 1: PREDICTIVE ANALYTICS, CLASSIFICATION & DECISION TREES (WEEK 7)

## 1.1 Foundations & Methodological Taxonomy of Predictive Analytics

### 1. Conceptual Definition & Core Objective
- **Core Question**: *"What will happen? What are the trends and forecasts?"*
- **Operational Mechanism**:
  $$\mathbf{\text{Historical Data} \longrightarrow \text{Pattern Identification} \longrightarrow \text{Mathematical Model} \longrightarrow \text{Future Forecast}}$$
  1. Extract structural patterns, trends, and co-movements from historical business records;
  2. Formalize those historical relationships into a mathematical algorithm or statistical model;
  3. Input new, hypothetical, or future time parameters to project unknown future operational states.

### 2. Quantitative vs. Qualitative Forecasting Approaches ⭐

| Dimension | Quantitative Forecasting (定量预测) | Qualitative Forecasting (定性预测) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Data Source** | Objective historical transaction logs, POS telemetry, ERP records, sensor metrics, and time series. | Subjective human expertise, executive intuition, customer qualitative feedback, and panel consensus. |
| **Core Methodologies** | • Simple and Multiple Linear Regression;<br>• Time-Series Decomposition & Trendlines;<br>• Machine Learning (Classification, Random Forest). | • Delphi Method (iterative expert consensus);<br>• Focus Groups & Consumer Surveys;<br>• Salesforce Composite Estimates. |
| **Operational Pros & Cons** | **Objective, mathematically repeatable, automated**; but requires clean, continuous historical data; cannot anticipate unprecedented black-swan shocks. | **Indispensable when launching entirely novel products with ZERO historical data**; but vulnerable to cognitive biases, optimism bias, and unquantifiable error bands. |

## 1.2 Classification Models & Supply Chain Applications ⭐⭐

### 1. The Nature of Classification
- **Definition**: The algorithmic process of establishing mapping rules based on training data to assign input objects into predefined categorical buckets (*"Creating a set of classes for data"*).
- **Two Fundamental Types**:
  - **Binary Classification (二分类)**: The target output consists of strictly **two discrete classes** (e.g., *Spam vs. Not Spam*, *Loan Approved vs. Rejected*, *Customer Will Churn vs. Retain*).
  - **Multiclass Classification (多分类)**: The target output consists of **three or more discrete classes** (e.g., *Supplier Risk Tier: Low, Medium, High, Critical*; *Customer Value Segments: Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum*).
- **Classic SCM Decision Benchmark (Slide 31)**:
  $$\mathbf{\text{Operational Problem: “Whether to sign a contract with this supplier?”} \implies \text{Yes / No (Binary Classification)}}$$
  - *Inputs*: Historical on-time delivery percentage, defective units per million, financial liquidity ratios, ESG compliance score.
  - *Output*: Binary recommendation generated by the algorithm (*Sign / Do Not Sign*).

### 2. Popular Classification Algorithms in SCM
1. **Decision Trees (决策树)**: Highly intuitive, visual, rules-based logic mirroring human decision flows; exceptional business interpretability.
2. **Random Forest (随机森林)**: An ensemble method combining dozens or hundreds of de-correlated decision trees through majority voting; high predictive accuracy, robust against overfitting.
3. **Voting Classifiers (投票分类器)**: An ensemble meta-model aggregating predictions from diverse algorithmic families (e.g., Logistic Regression + Support Vector Machines + Decision Trees) to decide the final class.
4. **Neural Networks & Deep Learning (神经网络与深度学习)**: Multi-layer perceptrons capable of mapping non-linear relationships across massive, high-dimensional datasets.

## 1.3 Decision Tree Architecture & Operational Terminology ⭐⭐⭐

> A Decision Tree is fundamentally an **upside-down tree-shaped diagram** used for classification and regression. It segments a heterogeneous population into increasingly homogeneous subgroups through an interconnected series of **Boolean tests (Yes/No)** or categorical splits.

```
                    [ Root Node (根节点: Initial Population & Starting Point) ]
                                            │
                        ┌───────────────────┴───────────────────┐
                   (Splitting / Branching: 基于最优特征进行属性划分)
                        ▼                                       ▼
             [ Parent / Child Node ]                 [ Parent / Child Node ]
                        │                                       │
                  ┌─────┴─────┐                                 │
                  ▼           ▼                                 ▼
             [Leaf Node] [Leaf Node]              [ Leaf / Terminal Node (叶节点: 最终输出类别) ]
```

### Core Anatomical Components (Slide 19)

| Component Name | Structural Function & Operational Role |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Root Node (根节点)** | The single starting node at the top of the tree; contains the entire, undivided baseline dataset. |
| **Splitting / Branching (分裂 / 分支)** | The process of dividing a node into two or more sub-nodes based on an optimal splitting criterion (e.g., Gini Impurity, Information Gain). |
| **Parent Node (父节点)** | Any hierarchical node that is subdivided into subsequent downstream child nodes. |
| **Child Node (子节点)** | Sub-nodes derived directly from the division of an upstream parent node. |
| **Leaf / Terminal Node (叶节点 / 终端节点)** ⭐ | **Nodes without further splits (终点节点)**; represents the final terminal prediction or assigned class outcome. |

# SECTION 2: REGRESSION MODELING & EXCEL OUTPUT INTERPRETATION

## 2.1 Theoretical Foundations of Regression Analysis

### 1. Classification vs. Regression in SCM ⭐
- **Classification (分类)**: Predicts a **discrete, categorical state or binary choice** (*"Sign or Do Not Sign"*, *"Default or Pay"*).
- **Regression (回归)**: Predicts a **continuous, numeric outcome variable** (*"What will demand volume be next quarter?"*, *"What should be the optimal dollar price for this product?"*).

### 2. Dependent vs. Independent Variables
- **Dependent Variable ($Y$) / Target / Response**: The passive metric we seek to explain, forecast, or optimize (e.g., Product Selling Price, Units Demanded).
- **Independent Variable ($X$) / Predictor / Explanatory**: The active driver features hypothesized to influence or explain changes in $Y$ (e.g., Production Cost, Inventory Holding Cost, Advertising Outlays).

### 3. Simple Linear Regression Model
$$\mathbf{y = a \cdot x + b + \varepsilon}$$
- $y$ = Dependent variable (因变量)
- $x$ = Independent variable (自变量)
- $a$ = **Slope (斜率)**: The average marginal change in $y$ associated with a one-unit increase in $x$.
- $b$ = **Intercept (截距)**: The baseline value of $y$ when $x = 0$.
- $\varepsilon$ = Random error term (随机误差项).
- **Ordinary Least Squares (OLS - 最小二乘法)**: Solves for the line of best fit by mathematically minimizing the sum of squared vertical deviations between actual observed data points and the fitted regression line.

## 2.3 The Three-Step Decision Rule for Excel Regression Output ⭐⭐⭐

> When evaluating an Excel regression summary table in an exam, execute this sequential three-step verification procedure:

```
[ Step 1: Check R Square ]        ──► Is R² between 0.50 and 0.99?
                  │
                  ▼
[ Step 2: Check Significance F ]  ──► Is Significance F < 0.05?
                  │                   (If NO ──► CANNOT PROCEED, STOP!)
                  ▼
[ Step 3: Check Coefficient P-val]──► Are individual P-values < 0.05?
                                      (Identify which predictors are significant)
```

### The Three Evaluation Criteria (Slides 54 & 55)

| Step | Metric | Official Evaluation Rule | Operational Interpretation |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Step 1** | **R Square ($R^2$)<br>(Coefficient of Determination)** | **Acceptable Range:<br>$0.50 \sim 0.99$ ($50\% \sim 99\%$)** ⭐ | Quantifies the percentage of total variance in the dependent variable explained by the independent variables. If $R^2 < 0.50$, explanatory power is insufficient. If $R^2 = 1.00$, suspect artificial data or extreme overfitting. |
| **Step 2** | **Significance F<br>(ANOVA Section)** | **Must be strictly LESS than $0.05$<br>($\text{Significance } F < 0.05$)** ⭐ | Serves as the omnibus P-value for the entire regression model. **If Significance F $\ge 0.05$, the model is statistically invalid; you CANNOT proceed with forecasting!** |
| **Step 3** | **P-value<br>(Coefficients Section)** | **Each predictor P-value must be $< 0.05$** ⭐ | Tests whether an individual predictor's slope is significantly different from zero. If a variable's P-value $\ge 0.05$, it exerts no statistically demonstrable effect on $Y$ and should be considered for removal. |

## 2.4 Time-Series Trendline & Forecasting (Airline Passenger Case p2.png) ⭐⭐

> **Empirical Context**: Historical monthly passenger volumes across 12 consecutive years ($12 \times 12 = 144$ months).  
> **Horizontal Axis ($x$)**: Continuous month index ($x = 1, 2, \dots, 144$).  
> **Vertical Axis ($y$)**: Total monthly passenger volume (in units).

```
[ Linear Trendline Equation ] : y = 2.6572 x + 87.653
[ Coefficient of Determination] : R² = 0.8536
```

### 1. Parameter Interpretation
- **Slope ($a = 2.6572$)**: Passenger traffic grows at a steady linear pace of approximately **2.66 passengers per additional month**.
- **Intercept ($b = 87.653$)**: Baseline theoretical passenger volume at time zero ($x = 0$).
- **$R^2 = 0.8536$**: Time progression accounts for **85.36% of the variation** in total monthly airline passenger volumes.

### 2. Forward Extrapolation & Forecast Calculation
- Historic dataset spans month 1 to month 144 (December of Year 12).
- **Forecast for January of Year 13 (Month index $x = 145$)**:
  $$\mathbf{y_{\text{Jan, Yr 13}} = (2.657184 \times 145) + 87.65278 = 385.2917 + 87.6528 = \mathbf{472.94\text{ passengers}}}$$
- **Forecast for February of Year 13 (Month index $x = 146$)**:
  $$\mathbf{y_{\text{Feb, Yr 13}} = (2.657184 \times 146) + 87.65278 = 387.9488 + 87.6528 = \mathbf{475.60\text{ passengers}}}$$

### 3. Trend vs. Seasonality Distinction
- **Long-term Trend (长期趋势)**: Captured by the upward sloping OLS regression line ($a > 0$).
- **Seasonality (季节性波动)**: The cyclical wave patterns oscillating around the straight line (recurring summer travel peaks and winter travel troughs repeated each year).

# SECTION 3: FORECASTING CHALLENGES, TRUST DEFICITS & THE BULLWHIP EFFECT

## 3.1 The Fundamental Barrier to SCM Forecasting: The Trust Problem ⭐⭐

> **Slide 63 Core Doctrine**:  
> *"**Biggest Problem is TRUST**: Who should optimise the supply chain and why? How the benefits will be shared? Is it fair? Who is taking advantage?"*

- **Information Holding / Hoarding (信息私藏)**: Trading partners withhold true cost structures, inventory levels, and unvarnished demand projections out of fear that shared data will be weaponized against them in commercial price negotiations.
- **Misaligned Incentives (利益分配失衡)**: If one firm invests heavily in end-to-end ERP integration while downstream partners capture all the cost savings, collaboration collapses back into fragmented local optimization.

---

## 3.2 The Bullwhip Effect Deep Dive ⭐⭐⭐

### 1. Definition & Core Phenomenon
- **The Bullwhip Effect**: The systemic phenomenon in supply chain networks where **small fluctuations in end-consumer retail demand become progressively amplified as orders propagate upstream** through retailers, distributors, manufacturers, and raw material suppliers.

```
[ End Consumer Demand ]  ──► Slight initial ripple: +5% retail sales increase
           │
           ▼
     [ Retailer ]        ──► Orders extra safety buffer: Places +10% order to wholesaler
           │
           ▼
    [ Wholesaler ]       ──► Adds distribution buffer: Places +15% order to manufacturer
           │
           ▼
   [ Manufacturer ]      ──► Overreacts with aggressive production schedule: Ramps output +25%
           │
           ▼
  [ Raw Material Tier ]  ──► Receives massively distorted demand signal: Over-mines / stockpiles
                             ──► SEVERE INDUSTRY-WIDE OVERCAPACITY & WRITE-DOWNS!
```

---

### 2. Five Operational Root Causes (Slide 64) ⭐⭐⭐

| # | Root Cause | Operational Mechanism & Distortion Driver |
| :-: | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Demand Forecasting<br>(需求预测多重更新)** | Each supply chain tier generates its own forecasts based on incoming purchase orders from immediate downstream buyers rather than actual consumer checkout data. Each tier layers on its own safety stock, compounding forecasting error upstream. |
| **2** | **Long Lead Times<br>(长交货提前期)** | Extended lead times expand the replenishment exposure window ($ROP = d \times L + SS$), requiring larger safety stock cushions and magnifying order volatility. |
| **3** | **Batch Ordering<br>(大批量周期性订货)** | Companies place large, infrequent orders to secure volume discounts or achieve full truckload (FTL) transport economies. This creates artificial, pulse-like order spikes interspersed with periods of zero demand. |
| **4** | **Price Fluctuations<br>(价格波动与促销诱发)** ⭐ | Periodic promotional discounts and trade deals encourage **Forward Buying (提前囤货)**—purchasing large quantities ahead of actual need. This produces artificial demand surges during promotions followed by demand troughs. |
| **5** | **Inflated Orders / Shortage Gaming<br>(虚假订货 / 短缺博弈)** ⭐ | When supply capacity is constrained, suppliers allocate available goods proportionally based on order sizes. Anticipating this rationing, buyers submit inflated phantom orders. Once capacity recovers, buyers cancel orders, leaving manufacturers with stranded inventory. |

---

### 3. Eight Strategies to Eliminate or Mitigate the Bullwhip Effect (Slide 68) ⭐⭐⭐

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                     Strategies to Counter the Bullwhip Effect                   │
│                                                                                 │
│  [ Information Transparency ] ──► Real-Time Information Sharing (POS Telemetry) │
│                                   Collaborative Planning (CPFR Framework)       │
│                                                                                 │
│  [ Operational Synchronization] ──► Vendor Managed Inventory (VMI Integration)  │
│                                   Smaller, Frequent Replenishment Batches       │
│                                   Lead Time Compression                         │
│                                                                                 │
│  [ Commercial Alignment ]     ──► Everyday Low Prices (EDLP Price Stability)    │
│                                   AI & Advanced Analytics Demand Sensing        │
│                                   Long-Term Trust-Based Strategic Alliances     │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Real-Time Information Sharing (实时信息共享)**:
   - Transmit point-of-sale (**POS scanner data**) and inventory levels up the chain via EDI or cloud APIs, providing visibility into true consumer demand.
2. **Collaborative Planning, Forecasting, and Replenishment - CPFR (协同规划、预测与补货)**:
   - A formal inter-organizational framework where trading partners share a single synchronized demand forecast and joint replenishment schedule.
3. **Vendor Managed Inventory - VMI (供应商管理库存)** ⭐:
   - **Core Mechanism**: Upstream vendors monitor the buyer's inventory consumption directly and take full responsibility for replenishment timing and quantities, bypassing distorted intermediate purchase orders.
4. **Smaller, Frequent Orders (小批量、高频次补货)**:
   - Transition away from massive episodic purchase batches; use consolidated mixed shipments or milk-runs to smooth order streams into continuous flows.
5. **Everyday Low Prices - EDLP (天天低价策略)**:
   - Pioneered by Wal-Mart; eliminates periodic promotional discounts to smooth consumer buying behavior and avoid forward-buying spikes.
6. **Lead Time Reduction (缩短全流程提前期)**:
   - Shorten manufacturing cycles, streamline customs clearances, and use regional distribution hubs to compress order-to-delivery lead times.
7. **Advanced Analytics & AI Forecasting (融合多维外部变量的 AI 需求预测)**:
   - Incorporate external demand drivers (weather forecasts, economic indicators, social sentiment) into machine learning forecasting models.
8. **Long-Term Strategic Relationships with Suppliers (深化长期战略互信)**:
   - Establish equitable cost-and-gain sharing contracts to overcome the trust problem and disincentivize opportunistic shortage gaming.

# SECTION 4: MASTER FORMULA SHEET & STATISTICAL DECISION THRESHOLDS (PART 8 SYNTHESIS)

## 4.1 Master SCM Formula Sheet ⭐⭐⭐

| Category | Model / Metric Name | Standard Mathematical Formula | Parameter Definitions & Units |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Inventory** | **Economic Order Quantity (EOQ)** | $$\mathbf{EOQ = \sqrt{\frac{2DS}{H}}}$$ | $D$: Annual Demand (units); $S$: Order/Setup Cost (\$/order); $H$: Holding Cost (\$/unit/year). **At EOQ: Annual Holding Cost $\equiv$ Annual Ordering Cost!** |
| **Inventory** | **Total Annual Inventory Cost (TAC)** | $$\mathbf{TAC = (D \cdot P) + \left(SS + \frac{Q}{2}\right)H + \left(\frac{D}{Q}\right)S}$$ | $P$: Unit purchase price; $SS$: Safety stock buffer; $Q$: Batch order quantity; Average Inventory $= SS + Q/2$. |
| **Inventory** | **Simple Safety Stock (SS)** | $$\mathbf{SS = \text{Max LT Usage} - \text{Avg LT Usage}}$$ | Difference between maximum usage during lead time and average usage during lead time. **Root cause: VARIATION.** |
| **Performance** | **Perfect Order Measurement (POM)** | $$\mathbf{POM = (\%Comp) \times (\%On\text{-}time) \times (\%Dmg\text{-}free) \times (\%Inv)}$$ | Multiplicative calculation across all four metrics (Complete, On-time, Damage-free, Invoiced correctly). **Never take an arithmetic average!** |
| **Performance** | **Cash-to-Cash Cycle (CCC)** | $$\mathbf{CCC = DIO + DSO - DPO}$$ | Days Inventory Outstanding $+$ Days Sales Outstanding $-$ Days Payable Outstanding. **Shorter is better; can be negative.** |
| **Performance** | **Inventory Turnover (IT)** | $$\mathbf{IT = \frac{\text{Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)}}{\text{Value of Average Inventory}}}$$ | Annual COGS divided by average inventory holding value. **Higher ratio reflects greater efficiency.** (YouRace: 12 $\rightarrow$ 15). |
| **Performance** | **Freight Cost per Unit** | $$\mathbf{\text{Unit Cost} = \frac{\text{Total Freight Incurred}}{\text{Total Units Shipped}}}$$ | Total transportation expenditure divided by total volume dispatched. |
| **Predictive** | **Simple Linear Regression** | $$\mathbf{y = a \cdot x + b + \varepsilon}$$ | $y$: Dependent variable; $x$: Independent predictor; $a$: Slope coefficient; $b$: Intercept constant; $\varepsilon$: Residual error. |
| **Predictive** | **Multiple Linear Regression** | $$\mathbf{Y = a_1 x_1 + a_2 x_2 + \dots + a_n x_n + b}$$ | $x_i$: Distinct independent variables; $a_i$: Partial regression coefficients (marginal sensitivity per dollar). |
| **Predictive** | **Linear Trendline Extrapolation** | $$\mathbf{y_t = a \cdot t + b}$$ | $t$: Sequential time index; $a$: Baseline growth rate per period. |

## 4.2 Statistical Decision Thresholds Quick Reference ⭐⭐⭐

| Statistical Test / Metric | Ideal / Good Range | Acceptable Boundary | Operational Rule & Violation Action |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Skewness (偏度)** | $[-1.0, +1.0]$ | $[-2.0, +2.0]$ | If $|Skewness| > 2.0 \implies$ Severe asymmetry; **Parametric tests invalid; switch to Non-Parametric tests.** |
| **Kurtosis (峰度)** | $[-2.0, +2.0]$ | $[-3.0, +3.0]$ | If $|Kurtosis| > 3.0 \implies$ Heavy tails/outliers; **Use Non-Parametric tests.** |
| **Pearson Correlation ($|r|$)** | $\pm 0.50 \sim \pm 1.00$ (High/Strong) | $\pm 0.30 \sim \pm 0.49$ (Moderate) | Below $\pm 0.29 \implies$ Small / Low correlation (weak linear association). |
| **Regression Coefficient ($R^2$)** | **$0.50 \sim 0.99$ ($50\% \sim 99\%$)** | $0.50 \sim 0.99$ | Below $0.50 \implies$ Inadequate model explanatory power; equal to $1.00 \implies$ Overfitting or fabricated data. |
| **Hypothesis Testing P-value** | $< 0.05$ | $< 0.05$ | If $P \ge 0.05 \implies$ **Fail to Reject $H_0$** (Insufficient evidence to reject null baseline). |
| **Regression Significance F** | $< 0.05$ | $< 0.05$ | If $\text{Significance } F \ge 0.05 \implies$ Model overall is not statistically significant; **CANNOT proceed.** |

## 4.3 Top 15 Must-Know Exam Pitfalls & High-Yield Hacks

1. **Hypothesis Mathematical Sign Rules**:
   - $H_0$ MUST contain an equality sign ($=, \le, \ge$); $H_1$ STRICTLY FORBIDDEN from having an equality sign ($<, >, \ne$).
2. **P-Value Decision Logic**:
   - $P < 0.05 \implies$ Reject $H_0$; $P \ge 0.05 \implies$ **Fail to Reject $H_0$** (Never say *"proved $H_0$ is true"*).
   - Definition: *"P-value is the chance of your result being just a coincidence."*
3. **Parametric vs. Non-Parametric Trigger Matchings**:
   - 2 related samples + non-normal $\implies$ **Wilcoxon signed rank test**.
   - 2 independent samples + non-normal (Likert scale) $\implies$ **Mann-Whitney U-test**.
   - 3+ independent samples + non-normal $\implies$ **Kruskal-Wallis H-test**.
   - 3+ related samples + non-normal $\implies$ **Friedman test**.
4. **Apache Spark Memory Advantage**:
   - Runs up to 100x faster than Hadoop MapReduce because it processes data **in-memory (RAM)** rather than writing intermediate steps to local disk storage.
5. **KNIME Core Competency**:
   - Drag-and-drop workflow tool specialized for **integrating data from numerous disparate sources** into a unified pipeline.
6. **Python vs. R Decision**:
   - Building large-scale software systems and operational pipelines from scratch $\implies$ **Python**. Specialized statistical modeling only $\implies$ **R**.
7. **The Root Reason for Safety Stock**:
   - The single root cause is **VARIATION** (demand, lead time, production rates, quality). If variation is zero, required safety stock is ZERO.
8. **Delayed Differentiation (Postponement) Cases**:
   - Smart TVs (delaying localized power cables and operating system language installation); Sofa manufacturing (pre-building generic base frames and delaying upholstery).
9. **Part Commonality Benchmark**:
   - The European Union universal **USB Type-C** mandate for handheld electronic devices.
10. **Servitisation Industry Flagship**:
    - **Rolls-Royce** generates $\approx 50\%$ of corporate revenues from Power-by-the-Hour engine maintenance services.
11. **Bubble Graph vs. Radar Chart**:
    - Three numerical dimensions (Cost vs. Value vs. Risk) $\implies$ **Bubble Graph**.
    - Multi-criteria capability profiling (area covered reflects overall value) $\implies$ **Radar / Spider Chart**.
12. **The Trust Problem in Forecasting**:
    - The biggest barrier to collaborative supply chain integration is **TRUST** (fear of data exploitation and inequitable gain sharing).
13. **Bullwhip Mitigation Mechanisms**:
    - **VMI** (vendor monitors actual shelf POS consumption); **CPFR** (joint demand forecasting); **EDLP** (eliminating forward-buying price spikes).
14. **Goldratt's Goal Definition of SCM**:
    - Maximizing **Throughput** (money generated through sales) while simultaneously reducing **Inventory** and **Operating Expense**.
15. **EOQ Cost Equivalence Theorem**:
    - At the calculated optimal EOQ order quantity, **Annual Holding Cost strictly equals Annual Ordering Cost**!

### Question 1
